About & sources

What this is. A reference map of notable state-level AI laws, pending bills and official guidance in the United States. Status is current as of October 14, 2026. It is not a complete list of every AI-related bill.

Sources. Every entry's first link is an official government source: the state legislature's bill page or enacted text, a governor's order or signing release, an attorney general, or a state agency. Where a second link appears, it is independent analysis (a law firm, news outlet or policy tracker) and is labeled with its publisher. Dates, numbers and court rulings in each summary were checked against the linked sources.

Method. Entries were compiled from state legislature records, executive and agency publications and reputable legal trackers, then checked individually against the official source. Summaries are written in our own words and are simplified; read the linked text for the precise legal requirements.

Job-risk context. The optional state shading and the exposure figures in state profiles come from Tufts University's Digital Planet: Chakravorti, Bhaskar et al., "When Wired Belts Become the New Rust Belts: AI and the Emerging Geography of American Job Risk," Digital Planet, The Fletcher School at Tufts University, March 17, 2026 (report and interactive site), using its March 2026 data booklet. Figures are the study's median adoption scenario: the estimated share of each state's jobs at risk of loss as AI is adopted. They are projections, not forecasts of actual job losses, and they vary widely across the study's slower and faster scenarios. Tufts does not publish industry figures by state, so state profiles show the state's most at-risk metro areas and the study's national industry figures. The figures are reproduced from Tufts' data; this map does not modify their methodology.

Political leaning. The optional red/blue shading shows each state's 2024 presidential result: the Republican candidate's share of the presidential vote minus the Democratic candidate's, as a percentage of all votes cast (R+ means the state voted Republican, D+ Democratic), from the Federal Election Commission's Official 2024 Presidential General Election Results. States are shown simply as red (voted Republican) or blue (voted Democratic); hover over a state for its margin. It describes how a state voted for president, not the party that controls its government or how its AI laws were passed.

Presidential vote + AI job risk. Red or blue shows how a state voted for president (FEC, 2024 official results); light, medium or dark shows its share of jobs at risk from AI (Tufts Digital Planet, median scenario), split into thirds of the 50 states. It shows where the two overlap, not that either one causes the other.

Republican vs. Democratic comparison. Both presidential-vote views include a panel comparing the two groups of states: the share of states that voted Republican and states that voted Democratic for president in 2024 that have enacted at least one law or adopted regulation in each topic on this map. Shares are used because the groups differ in size (31 and 19 states). The map lists notable measures rather than every bill, so the comparison describes what is on this map.

No preparedness score. State profiles deliberately do not rate how prepared a state is. Legislation is only one part of a state's response; budgets, workforce programs, education systems and private-sector action are not captured here.

Map data. State outlines from the us-atlas project (U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries). Base map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Categories and fields. Map colors group entries by legal force: Enacted (statutes, local laws and adopted regulations, whether or not yet in effect), Pending (bills and proposed regulations) and Other action (executive orders, attorney general guidance, agency policies, programs, studies, investigations and resolutions). Each entry also lists its instrument type, its current stage, its effective date and any litigation, as stated in its sources, plus the date it was last checked against them.

What the counts mean. Counts are of entries on this map, not of distinct laws: a few entries combine related measures, and one act can appear under more than one topic. The map lists notable measures, not every AI-related bill. Alaska, West Virginia and Wyoming have no entries recorded here; that does not mean they have no AI legislation.

Verification limits. Each entry was checked against its linked sources, and the data was revised against official texts on October 14, 2026. Some official sites block automated access, so not every page could be retrieved automatically, and a successful check does not guarantee every detail of every summary. Read the linked official text before relying on an entry.

Not legal advice. This map is for general information only. Laws change frequently, and effective dates, amendments and litigation can affect whether a provision applies. Consult the official text or a qualified attorney before relying on it.
