About & sources

What this is. A reference map of notable state-level AI laws, pending bills and official guidance in the United States. Status is current as of October 13, 2026. It is not a complete list of every AI-related bill.

Sources. Every entry's first link is an official government source: the state legislature's bill page or enacted text, a governor's order or signing release, an attorney general, or a state agency. Where a second link appears, it is independent analysis (a law firm, news outlet or policy tracker) and is labeled with its publisher. Dates, numbers and court rulings in each summary were checked against the linked sources.

Method. Entries were compiled from state legislature records, executive and agency publications and reputable legal trackers, then checked individually against the official source. Summaries are written in our own words and are simplified; read the linked text for the precise legal requirements.

Job-risk context. The optional state shading and the exposure figures in state profiles come from Tufts University's Digital Planet: Chakravorti, Bhaskar et al., "When Wired Belts Become the New Rust Belts: AI and the Emerging Geography of American Job Risk," Digital Planet, The Fletcher School at Tufts University, March 17, 2026 (report and interactive site), using its March 2026 data booklet. Figures are the study's median adoption scenario: the estimated share of each state's jobs at risk of loss as AI is adopted. They are projections, not forecasts of actual job losses, and they vary widely across the study's slower and faster scenarios. Tufts does not publish industry figures by state, so state profiles show the state's most at-risk metro areas and the study's national industry figures. The figures are reproduced from Tufts' data; this map does not modify their methodology.

Political leaning. The optional red/blue shading shows each state's 2024 presidential result: Donald Trump's share of the vote minus Kamala Harris's, as a percentage of all votes cast, from the Federal Election Commission's Official 2024 Presidential General Election Results. Darker means a wider margin; the scale is capped at 30 points. It describes how a state voted for president, not the party that controls its government or how its AI laws were passed.

"Is the response where the risk is?" chart. Plots each state by its share of jobs at risk from AI (Tufts) and the strongest AI-and-workforce measure it has on this map: none; executive actions, programs or studies only; bills pending; or a law enacted. Dot color shows the 2024 presidential result (FEC) and dot size the number of jobs at risk. The headline figures are calculated from these same data. The chart shows alignment between exposure and response, not cause and effect: the job-risk estimates predate nearly all of the measures.

No preparedness score. State profiles deliberately do not rate how prepared a state is. Legislation is only one part of a state's response; budgets, workforce programs, education systems and private-sector action are not captured here.

Map data. State outlines from the us-atlas project (U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries). Base map © OpenStreetMap contributors.

Not legal advice. This map is for general information only. Laws change frequently, and effective dates, amendments and litigation can affect whether a provision applies. Consult the official text or a qualified attorney before relying on it.

What this does and doesn't show. It shows whether states' responses line up with their exposure. It can't show whether laws or politics change job risk: Tufts estimates risk from each state's mix of jobs as of May 2024, and most of these measures were adopted in 2025–26 and take effect in 2026–27, so they could not have affected those estimates. Columns show the strongest workforce measure a state has on this map; the map covers notable measures, not every bill. Darker states and higher job risk also go together partly because of the kind of jobs in each state, not party alone.
